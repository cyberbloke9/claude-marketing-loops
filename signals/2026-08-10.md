## Signal 1: The government's 4-month builder extension just went from a Delhi advisory to an actual order in your state — and it quietly shrinks your compensation claim by the same four months

- **What the data shows:** On 7 Aug 2026, MahaRERA issued its own order granting a blanket four-month extension to the completion timelines of registered Maharashtra housing projects whose original, revised, or extended completion date falls on or after 28 Feb 2026 — invoking the force-majeure provision (Section 6) of the RERA Act over the West Asia conflict's disruption to construction-material supply chains, per the 31 Jul 2026 MoHUA advisory. The relief is automatic — promoters do not file a separate application — but projects registered on or after 1 Aug 2026 are excluded. Telangana's TG-RERA issued a parallel order around the same window, automatically extending the completion deadline to 30 Nov 2026 for eligible projects whose timeline fell between 28 Feb and 31 Jul 2026, again with no individual application or fee. Business Standard's 3 Aug 2026 analysis of the advisory's mechanics flags the trade-off explicitly: developers will not be treated as "in default" for the four-month window if the extension is granted, which "can help prevent a wave of stalled projects" and shield them from insolvency risk — but "the window during which buyers can claim delay compensation is reduced for the extended period." Sources: [Moneylife (MahaRERA, 7 Aug 2026)](https://www.moneylife.in/article/west-asia-conflict-maharera-grants-4month-extension-to-eligible-housing-projects/81292.html), [Punekar News (MahaRERA)](https://www.punekarnews.in/maharera-announces-four-month-project-deadline-extension-due-to-west-asia-conflict/), [Newsmeter (TG-RERA)](https://newsmeter.in/hyderabad/hyderabad-tgrera-extends-registration-validity-of-real-estate-projects-homebuyers-to-benefit-772781), [Telangana Today (TG-RERA)](https://telanganatoday.com/tgrera-extends-validity-for-real-estate-projects-amid-west-asia-crisis), [Business Standard, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/rera-extension-may-shield-stalled-housing-projects-from-insolvency-risk-126080301213_1.html).
- **Why now:** direct continuation of last loop's Signal 1 (flagged REACTIVE, the 31 Jul 2026 MoHUA advisory) — this is what the advisory actually became once two state regulators wrote their own orders, with MahaRERA's order landing just three days before this report. Not re-flagged reactive (the "policy change" lane was used on this trigger last loop); the news value this loop is the state-level mechanics and the buyer-side cost the coverage so far has undersold.
- **Exclusivity:** LOW (public regulatory orders) — TERREM's synthesis is the trade-off nobody's headline leads with: every state order that "protects" a builder from default status also deletes four months from the compensation clock a delayed buyer was accumulating. The checkable angle: a buyer can calculate their own foregone delay-interest for the exact four-month window once their state RERA's order is published, and should do that math before treating the extension as good news.
- **Persona × pain:** P1×DELIVERY (completion-assurance protection diluted, now with concrete state-level mechanics), P2×RERA (regulatory-risk parameters have moved — the extension is now a real, calculable financial fact, not a proposal)

## Signal 3: Gurugram just moved past fines. The district administration is now seizing ₹296 crore directly from 10-plus builders who ignored HRERA.

- **What the data shows:** The Gurugram district administration has initiated formal recovery proceedings against more than 10 real-estate developers over roughly ₹296 crore in unpaid penalties across approximately 380 cases in which HRERA had already found the developers failed to hand over possession within their promised timelines; individual penalties in the case set range from ₹25 lakh to ₹2 crore. HRERA declared the builders defaulters after repeated compliance notices went unheeded and referred the dues to the district administration for recovery under applicable revenue-recovery provisions, following a review meeting chaired by the Gurugram Deputy Commissioner that directed the revenue department to compile developer-wise dues and expedite recovery. Affected buyers span projects in Sectors 109, 65, 63A, 68, 95, 103 and 104. Sources: [ETRealty, via Unispace mirror](https://unispaceae.com/over-10-realtors-face-recovery-over-rs-296-crore-unpaid-rera-dues-etrealty/), [ABC Buildcon](https://www.abcbuildcon.in/insights/haryana-rera-recovers-296-crore-from-defaulting-builders), [Constrofacilitator](https://constrofacilitator.com/hrera-dues-recovery-begins-against-haryana-developers/), reported in the first week of August 2026.

The project was originally due for completion by 30 Mar 2024, later extended to 30 Mar 2026. ## Signal 5: Bengaluru's building approvals have quietly stopped moving — and even the ones that already moved rarely end in a legal occupancy certificate

- **What the data shows:** Per Deccan Herald reporting on 3 Aug 2026 (Naveen Menezes), approvals for development plans, change-of-land-use (CLU) requests, building plans, and occupancy certificates have "virtually come to a standstill" at the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) and Karnataka's Urban Development Department, despite robust underlying demand — developers report the clearance slowdown is disrupting project launches and raising costs as files continue migrating from the dissolved BBMP's records into the GBA's new five-corporation structure. This compounds a pre-existing, separately documented gap: an HN Justice Nagmohan Das Commission probe found the erstwhile BBMP had approved 36,236 building plans by May 2025 but only 1,504 of those buildings — about 4.15% — had actually obtained the occupancy certificate required for lawful habitation (Deccan Herald, 7 Sep 2025). Sources: [Deccan Herald, "Approval logjam leaves Bengaluru real estate sector in limbo," 3 Aug 2026](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/approval-logjam-leaves-bengaluru-real-estate-sector-in-limbo-4096886), [Deccan Herald, "Over 95% of BBMP-approved buildings lack occupancy certificates, finds probe," 7 Sep 2025](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/over-95-of-bbmp-approved-buildings-lack-occupancy-certificates-finds-probe-3716529).
- **Why now:** finally fills the Bengaluru/Karnataka slot flagged open on the last two loops' watchlists — the freshest dated, specific, sourced Bengaluru real-estate regulatory item this sweep, and a genuinely different risk category (pre-construction approval risk, not a post-possession delay-interest case) than anything run for any city in recent loops.
- **Exclusivity:** LOW (public reporting) — TERREM's synthesis is the compounding: a buyer eyeing a new Bengaluru launch faces two separate, sequential regulatory chokepoints — getting the project approved at all (currently stalled) and, even after approval and construction, actually getting the OC that makes possession legal (already failing for ~96% of approved buildings pre-logjam). The approval slowdown doesn't fix the second problem; if anything a backlog raises the odds corners get cut later to clear it.
- **Persona × pain:** P1×DELIVERY/QUALITY (a launch that isn't even approved yet carries both timeline risk and, on Bengaluru's own recent record, legal-occupancy risk down the line), P2×YIELD (fewer approved new launches is a supply-side signal worth tracking against locality pricing)
