## Signal 1: The Supreme Court just ruled — if your builder goes bankrupt, you don't inherit the bill for its "past sins"

- **What the data shows:** In a judgment delivered 3 September 2026 (*The Authorised Representative for Granite Gate Properties Pvt Ltd v. New Okhla Industrial Development Authority & Ors.*, 2026 INSC 952, Civil Appeal No. 3132 of 2026), a bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran set aside a July 2025 NCLAT order that had directed Noida homebuyers and a resolution applicant to pay the Noida Authority's "time-extension" charges as part of the insolvency costs of a defaulted developer. The underlying projects — Lotus Boulevard and Lotus Panache in Noida, developed by Granite Gate Properties — went into Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) after the builder defaulted; homebuyers themselves constituted the Committee of Creditors and pooled their own money under a CoC-approved "Pool and Build" mechanism to keep construction going, while SMV Agencies Pvt Ltd was later approved as the resolution applicant. The Court held that homebuyers and the successful resolution applicant "cannot be penalised for the developer's past sins," especially when the stalled project is being completed with the buyers' own pooled funds. Sources: [Business Standard, "Homebuyers can't be penalised for developer's default: Supreme Court", 4 Sept 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/homebuyers-can-t-be-penalised-for-developer-s-default-supreme-court-126090400266_1.html), [LiveLaw, "'Homebuyers Can't Be Penalised For Builders' Default': Supreme Court Rejects NOIDA's Time-Extension Charges In CIRP"](https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/homebuyers-cant-be-penalised-for-builders-default-supreme-court-rejects-noidas-time-extension-charges-in-cirp-548528), [Verdictum, case citation and bench detail, 2026 INSC 952](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/the-authorised-representative-v-ms-new-okhla-industrial-development-authority-2026-insc-952-1621395), [Bar & Bench, "Can penalties imposed on insolvent developer be recovered from homebuyers as CIRP costs? Supreme Court answers"](https://www.barandbench.com/news/can-penalties-imposed-on-insolvent-developer-be-recovered-from-homebuyers-as-cirp-costs-supreme-court-answers).
- **Why now:** the judgment itself landed 3 Sept 2026, four days inside this sweep — the Supreme Court's own precedent-setting resolution of a builder-default/CIRP fact pattern (an insolvent Noida developer, buyers pooling funds to finish their own homes) that TERREM's watchlist has tracked as reactive-lane-eligible since the MHADA/Bandra case first appeared three loops ago. This clears the reactive bar as the "major ruling" trigger: it is the apex court's final word on who bears the cost when a builder defaults mid-construction, a question that sits underneath every stalled-project story TERREM covers.
- **Exclusivity:** LOW (public Supreme Court judgment) — TERREM's synthesis: most coverage frames this narrowly as a Noida Authority/NCLAT technicality. The buyer-facing takeaway is broader and reassuring in a market full of stalled-project fear: when a builder collapses into insolvency and buyers themselves fund completion, the Court has now said those buyers — and whoever takes over the project — cannot also be handed the defaulting builder's own regulatory penalty bill. That reallocates real financial risk away from exactly the buyers TERREM's P1 cohort represents.
- **Persona × pain:** P1×DELIVERY (98% demand completion assurance; this ruling directly changes what "worst case" costs a buyer in a stalled project), P2×EXIT/RERA (a national precedent on liability allocation in CIRP-stuck projects — relevant to any investor holding units in an insolvent developer's project, in any of TERREM's six metros)

## Signal 2: Bengaluru homebuyers have won ₹1,081 crore in RERA orders. They've actually been paid ₹110 crore.

- **What the data shows:** A Deccan Herald investigation published 6 September 2026 found that RERA orders worth ₹1,081 crore issued in favour of Bengaluru homebuyers remain largely unrecovered — only around ₹110 crore has actually reached buyers, leaving roughly ₹971 crore stuck between what K-RERA has ordered and what homebuyers have received. Per K-RERA's own data (the most recent cumulative figure published, current through February 2026), close to 2,325 homebuyers have been granted relief out of 6,111 total complaints registered over the last five years — but only 312 of those complainants have actually been paid. The structural cause: RERA can quantify what a defaulting builder owes, but cannot itself seize a bank account or land — enforcement is handed to the state revenue department (recoverable as "arrears of land revenue" under RERA Act Section 40), which the report describes as a machinery "never built for real estate disputes." The same article places this alongside the Supreme Court's own 12 February 2026 floor remarks (Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi) that RERA "helps the defaulting builders and not homebuyers" and that, absent reform, the institution might as well be abolished — and notes the identical enforcement gap playing out for Delhi-NCR homebuyers. Sources: [Deccan Herald, "Rs 1.10 crore awarded, not a rupee paid: Why RERA orders are failing homebuyers", 6 Sept 2026](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/rs-110-crore-awarded-not-a-rupee-paid-why-rera-orders-are-failing-homebuyers-4136601), [Inventiva, "Homebuyers Win ₹1,081 Crore From Developers. They Get Just ₹110 Crore.", 6 Sept 2026](https://www.inventiva.co.in/trends/homebuyers-win-%E2%82%B91081-crore-from-developers-they-get-just-%E2%82%B9110-crore-what-happens-after-rera-delivers-its-verdict/).
- **Why now:** the investigative piece itself published 6 Sept 2026, inside this sweep, and lands one day after Signal 1's Supreme Court ruling on builder-default liability — together the two stories bracket the same underlying question (who actually pays, and does an order become money) from opposite ends: liability allocation (Signal 1) and enforcement failure (Signal 2). Note the underlying K-RERA aggregate is a "through February 2026" cumulative snapshot — the freshness here is the analysis and its Supreme Court framing, not a newly-updated dataset; disclosed for transparency.
- **Exclusivity:** LOW-MEDIUM (public K-RERA data + investigative synthesis) — TERREM's synthesis: buyers chase a RERA order as the finish line. The actual finish line, per this data, is roughly a 10% recovery rate — the order is a legal finding, not a bank transfer, and the gap between the two is a structural feature (a revenue-department bottleneck) rather than an exception. The checkable question for any buyer holding or considering a RERA complaint isn't "will I win" — most who complain plausibly can — it's "what happens after I win," which this data answers directly.
- **Persona × pain:** P1×DELIVERY/RERA ("tell me if this builder actually delivers" — RERA relief itself is not delivery), P2×EXIT/RERA (quantifies actual regulatory-recovery risk, not just headline builder-risk, for NCR and Bengaluru alike)
